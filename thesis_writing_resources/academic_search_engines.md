# Academic Search Engines and Databases

As a researcher, leveraging academic search engines is essential for conducting a comprehensive literature review. Below is a curated list of powerful search engines and databases to find academic journals, chapters, conference papers, and reports.

## Major Search Engines
* **[Google Scholar](http://scholar.google.com/)**: A free academic search engine indexing academic information from various online resources.
* **[GetCITED](http://www.getcited.org/)**: An online academic database indexing academic journals and citations, covering chapters, conference papers, reports, and presentations.
* **[Microsoft Academic Research](http://academic.research.microsoft.com/)**: A top search engine for academic resources.
* **[CiteSeerx](http://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu)**: A digital library and online academic journal catering specifically to the field of computer science.
* **[Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ)](http://www.doaj.org/)**: A search engine for free, open-access scientific and scholarly resources.
* **[PLOS ONE](http://www.plosone.org/)**: A free-access platform for science-related information (founded in 2006).
* **[ScienceDirect](http://www.sciencedirect.com/)**: A leading full-text scientific database offering journal articles and book chapters from over 2,500 journals and nearly 20,000 books.

## Additional Search Engines
* **Academic Info**: An in-depth directory of useful links and resources categorized by specific subject areas.
* **iSeek**: Widely used for academic research, designed specifically with students, teachers, and scholars in mind.
* **Virtual LRC (Virtual Learning Resources Center)**: Indexes thousands of academic websites, allowing exploration of educational sites with high-quality information.
* **Refseek**: A minimalist academic search engine that removes non-academic results, focusing strictly on science, academia, and research.
