# Plagiarism and Citation Decision Guide

Understanding when to cite and what constitutes plagiarism is fundamental to academic integrity. Reference this guide when writing your thesis to ensure your work is original and properly attributed.

## Part 1: Should I Cite It? (Decision Flowchart)

Follow this logic to determine if a citation is required for a piece of information in your text:

1. **Is it a direct quote?** -> **YES: CITE IT!** (Use quotation marks, author name, page/paragraph number).
2. **Is it a paraphrase?** (Discussing another person's work in your own words) -> **YES: CITE IT!**
3. **Is it someone else's data, image, or chart?** -> **YES: CITE IT!**
4. **Is it another person's idea or theory?** -> **YES: CITE IT!**
5. **Is it your own original idea OR common knowledge?** -> **NO NEED TO CITE (usually).**

**Important Exceptions / "However" Clauses:**
* **Auto-Plagiarism:** If you submitted your "own original idea/work" in a previous assignment or publication, **you must cite yourself!** Reusing previous work without citation is auto-plagiarism.
* **Inspired Ideas:** If you are using your own words, but the thought was inspired by another's idea, **cite it!**
* **Common Knowledge Verification:** Common knowledge refers to facts expected to be known by many people. Make sure it is truly common knowledge to everyone, not just to you because you've studied the topic extensively.

## Part 2: Did I Plagiarize? Types & Severity of Violations

Plagiarism isn't just copying and pasting an entire paper. It exists on a spectrum of severity. Below are common violations, ranked from most to least severe:

* **Identity Theft (Insanely Severe):** Stealing, copying, or purchasing another person's entire document and taking full credit.
* **Copycat (Extremely Severe):** Copying large portions (entire paragraphs/sections) of another's work without giving credit.
* **Cherry-pick (Terribly Severe):** Changing a few terms/phrases but keeping the rest of the text and ideas relatively unchanged without giving credit.
* **Mitosis (Immensely Severe):** Reusing large portions of your own work for another purpose without citing yourself.
* **Recycle (Profoundly Severe):** Duplicating an entire work of your own for another purpose without self-citation.
* **Remix (Hugely Severe):** Paraphrasing multiple sources and stitching them together to sound like your own original thought.
* **Ghost Citation (Very, Very Severe):** Citing a source that doesn't exist, or making up what the source actually said.
* **Half-n-half (Very Severe):** Citing many sources correctly, but completely failing to cite others at all.
* **Warp (Remarkably Severe):** Misinterpreting an author or citing a source out of context, distorting the original meaning.
* **Mosaic (Quite Severe):** Citing everything correctly but using very little of your own original thought.
* **Reflection (Notably Severe):** Citing everything correctly, but the organization, style, or layout of your work still too closely mimics the original work.
* **Miscue (Somewhat Severe):** Making a technical mistake in your citation (wrong author, wrong year).
* **Half-hearted (Mildly Severe):** Mostly citing correctly, but getting sloppy and leaving out small details like page numbers or publishers.

**The Golden Rule:** If you are confident that what you have written is entirely original in concept, style, structure, and diction—or if you have used another's ideas and rigorously cited the original source so a reader could locate it—you have not plagiarized.
