# Ways To Improve Scientific Writing For Non-native English Speakers

English is the language of science and mastering it is critical for your scientific career. Improving your written English can significantly boost the credibility of your academic papers and create a better impression among peers and colleagues. Here are five practical tips for non-native English speakers to improve their scientific writing:

## 1. Cut Down On The Apostrophes
There’s a growing over-emphasis on apostrophes in written English. Remember that an apostrophe symbolizes possession (e.g., "Ahmed's experiment") or a contraction (e.g., "there's"). Do not use apostrophes for plural items.
* **Incorrect:** The beaker's were clean.
* **Correct:** The beakers were clean.

## 2. Sometimes You Use "the" and Sometimes You Don't
The definite article "the" can be tricky because its use varies between languages. While "the" is used to refer to specific nouns, it is often dropped in English when referring to abstract concepts, fields of study, or general items.
* **Incorrect:** "in the biology" 
* **Correct:** "in biology"
Note: There are also regional differences (e.g., British English uses "attending university", while American English often uses "attending the university").

## 3. Know When To Round Numbers
When reporting measurements, experimental data, or mathematical equations, numbers must be exact.
* **Example:** "0.453 liters" or "24.67 seconds" are essential in documentation.
However, in casual conversations or informal contexts, using overly specific numbers is unnecessary.
* **Example:** Don't say "call me in 8.34 minutes"; just say "call me back in 10 minutes."

## 4. Understand Particular "-isms" (Colloquialisms)
Certain words have different meanings in different English-speaking regions. For example:
* In the US, "call" typically means to phone someone.
* In some regions of Europe (like Ireland), "call down" might mean to physically go visit someone's office.
Learn to recognize these quirks. Miscommunications are learning experiences, but in formal scientific writing, try to stick to universally understood terminology.

## 5. Be Mindful of British vs. American English
There are minor spelling differences between British and American English (e.g., "colour" vs. "color", "apologise" vs. "apologize"). One is not better than the other, but it is important to be consistent within your document. Additionally, make it easy for your international peers by using universal measurement systems wherever possible.

## Final Thoughts
* **Embrace Mistakes:** "Sin errores, no hay progreso" (without mistakes, there’s no progress). Don't just "play it safe" by repeating the same phrases. 
* **Don't Over-Apologize:** If you have an advanced understanding of the language, don't apologize for your English skills ahead of time. Often, readers won't even notice you are a non-native speaker.
* **Be Confident:** Be proud that you are working hard to improve your scientific writing in English!
