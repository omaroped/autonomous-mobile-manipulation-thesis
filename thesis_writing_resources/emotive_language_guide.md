# Emotive Language in Essay and Thesis Writing: Do’s and Don’ts
Languages are an essential part of our lives. The words we speak or write can calm, influence, motivate, irritate, or enrage another person. Emotive language is a type of language in which a set of words is purposefully chosen to elicit emotions. This language has a persuasive nature, detracting from the text’s formality and making it appear subjective rather than objective.

## What is Emotive Language
Emotive language is simple and accessible words that reach the target audience and can bring an emotional response in the reader or listener. 
- **Positive Behavior:** Words expressing good emotions (e.g., happiness, joy, cheer).
- **Negative Behavior:** Words expressing bad emotions (e.g., sadness, unhappiness, dejected).
- **Neutral/Context-dependent:** Words bringing a neutral reaction, connecting with an audience on a deeper emotional level.

### Examples
- **Positive:** Overjoyed, ecstatic, blissful, grateful, elated.
- **Negative:** Sad, angry, frustrated, disgusted, anxious, nervous.
- **Neutral:** Anticipative, skeptical, calm, indifferent, detached, objective.

## Why Avoid Emotive Language in Academic/Essay Writing
In thesis writing (or formal writing), emotive language should generally be avoided. It gives the impression that a piece of writing is subjective rather than objective. Essential reasons to avoid it:

1. **It Might Play on the Reader’s Feelings:** Never write something that can leave a sensational impact on the reader’s mind when objective knowledge is required.
2. **Evokes Strong Emotional Responses:** Unnecessary emphasis can create anger or shock and hurt sentiments.
3. **Might Create Misunderstanding:** Readers might misunderstand your point—especially important to avoid in scientific/formal texts.
4. **Makes Content Less Professional:** Academic writing is formal. Using emotive language can appear informal, reducing the quality of the communication.
5. **Diminishes Debates:** Supplying proof to back up your claims is key. Excessive dramatic language can give the impression that an argument is manipulated or weak.

## Tips to Avoid Using Emotive Language in Academic Writing
1. **Proofread to Find Errors:** Editing ensures that all triggering words are removed and improves overall quality. Maintain a neutral tone and use an active voice.
2. **Never Use Subjective Opinions:** Avoid using personal pronouns. Essay/thesis writing is an integral part of academics and not a performance of personal writing.
3. **Start with a Clear Approach:** Have a clear mind about your argument. Knowing your topic prevents using emotive words out of frustration or lack of clarity.
4. **Avoid Rephrasing:** Simply changing an emotive word to another synonym won't fix the issue. You need to change the sentence's subjective meaning back to an objective fact.
