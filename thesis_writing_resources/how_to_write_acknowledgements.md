# How to write an acknowledgement for a thesis: Full guide & Examples

The acknowledgements is a section in a thesis or dissertation where you thank individuals and organizations that supported your research. It is a way to express gratitude for academic, financial, or personal contributions. Unlike other parts, a thesis acknowledgement is more personal and is usually written in the first person.

Thesis acknowledgements take no more than 1 page and are dedicated to those who influenced your work, either professionally or personally. Here are the examples of individuals and entities you might express gratitude to:
- Academic supervisors, advisors, or mentors
- Research collaborators or colleagues
- Funding organizations or scholarship providers
- Institutions that provided resources or facilities
- Professors, teachers or instructors
- Family, friends, or loved ones who inspired you.

## Where to put acknowledgements in a thesis?
The acknowledgement section is placed near the beginning of your thesis, typically after the abstract and before the table of contents. This way, your acknowledgements will be easy to find, but the main content will remain uninterrupted.

## Whom should you thank in thesis acknowledgements?
Start with academic contributors, such as your supervisor, advisor, committee members, and colleagues who provided guidance or feedback. Next, acknowledge institutional contributors, including universities, funding bodies, libraries, research labs, and technical assistants. Finally, thank personal contributors, such as family, friends, or mentors who offered emotional support.

## Types of acknowledgements
- **Formal:** Focuses on professional and academic contributors with a formal tone, highlighting those who directly supported your research.
- **Informal:** More casual and personal, with a relaxed tone, often unsuitable for strictly professional settings.
- **Combined:** Blends formal and informal tones, balancing professionalism with personal gratitude.

## How to write an acknowledgement page for a thesis?
1. **Make a list of contributors:** Separate your contributors into professional and personal.
2. **Start with professional acknowledgements:** Begin with your supervisor, advisor, and thesis committee members. Use full names and titles.
3. **Acknowledge institutional support:** Universities, research labs, funding agencies, etc.
4. **Include personal gratitude:** Family, friends, and mentors who supported you.
5. **Conclude your thesis acknowledgements:** End with a final note of gratitude that ties everything together.
6. **Proofread:** Ensure the tone is consistent, grammar is correct, and double-check spelling of names, titles, and institutions.

## Examples
### Formal tone
"I am deeply grateful to my primary advisor, Dr. Emily Johnson, for her steadfast guidance and constructive feedback, which shaped the foundation of this research. Her mentorship and encouragement throughout each stage of this journey have been invaluable. I would also like to extend my sincere thanks to the members of my advisory committee, Dr. Michael Lee and Dr. Karen Taylor, for their insightful questions and thoughtful suggestions, which helped refine the scope and depth of this thesis. I am equally thankful to the Department of Environmental Studies at Greenfield University for providing the necessary resources and administrative support to carry out this study..."

### Combined tone
"I would like to express my sincere gratitude to my advisor, Dr. Emily Johnson, for her expert guidance and unwavering support throughout this journey... I am deeply grateful to the Department of Sociology at Greenfield University for providing the resources... Heartfelt thanks go to my family, especially my partner, Alex, for their constant encouragement... To my parents, John and Mary, thank you for your unwavering belief in my abilities..."
