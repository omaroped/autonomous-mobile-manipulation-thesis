# Pass 9 — Exhaustive System Audit (The DNA Map)

This final extended audit provides a comprehensive mapping of every control parameter, physics constant, and strategic heuristic in the project.

## 1. Advanced Control & Motion Logic

### A. Phase-Based Orchestration (`limo_pick_place.py`)
The system follows a rigid but robust timed-sequential state machine:
1.  **NAVIGATE**: Reactive visual servoing (P-control).
2.  **PRE_GRASP**: Simultaneous arm lift (`PRE_GRASP` pose) and gripper opening. Wait 3.0s.
3.  **GRASP**: Linear descent to `GRASP` pose. Wait 3.5s.
4.  **GRIP**: Trigger `GRIPPER_CLOSE`. Wait 1.0s for physics to settle.
5.  **LIFT**: Ascend to `LIFT` pose. Wait 2.0s.
6.  **CARRY**: Tuck arm into `CARRY` pose for base transport.
*   **Engineering Insight:** The long wait times (3.0s - 3.5s) are deliberate "dampers" to ensure the myCobot's low-rigidity links stop oscillating before the next high-precision phase.

### B. Kinematic Compensation (`limo_pick_place.py`)
*   **Rotation Bias:** All poses use `J1 = -1.5708`. 
*   **The "Why":** The arm was physically mounted with a 90° CCW rotation. The code compensates for this in every coordinate transform, ensuring the "forward" planning direction aligns with the robot's camera.
*   **Orientation Constraint:** `J2 + J3 + J4 ≈ -1.57` is maintained across all pick poses to ensure the gripper remains normal to the table.

## 2. Physics & Simulation Engineering

### C. The "Pin and Weld" Helpers (`base_pin.py`, `grasp_attacher.py`)
*   **Base Pinning:** Holds the LIMO model at a fixed world pose at **50 Hz**. This bypasses the Gazebo friction instability that causes "creeping" during manipulation.
*   **The "Pseudo-Weld" (Grasp Attacher):** Since Gazebo Classic lacks a robust ROS 2 vacuum gripper plugin, this node implements a **Coordinate Follower**. 
    *   **Algorithm:** $P_{box}(t) = P_{gripper}(t) + R_{base} \cdot \vec{off}_{initial}$.
    *   **Logic:** It locks the relative offset between the box and the gripper's TCP at the moment of contact, then updates the box's world pose at **25 Hz**.

### D. Inertial Properties (`limo_ackerman_base.xacro`)
*   **Base Mass:** 2.1557 kg.
*   **Wheel Mass:** 0.5 kg.
*   **Friction/Damping:** Wheel dynamics are hard-coded to `damping="0.1" friction="0.05"`. This is the "Goldilocks" setting identified in Pass 1 to prevent Ackermann sliding while maintaining drive torque.

## 3. Motion Planning (MoveIt/OMPL)

### E. The Planner Selection (`ompl_planning.yaml`)
*   **Primary Planner:** `RRTConnect`.
*   **Constraint:** The `longest_valid_segment_fraction` is set to **0.005** (default is 0.01).
*   **Effect:** This doubles the resolution of collision checking along the path, which is critical for the "narrow gap" top-down pick where the gripper passes within 1.3 cm of the table.

### F. Reachability Discovery (`reach_sweep.py`)
*   **Experiment Design:** A 9x5x9 grid search ($n=405$) across 5 orientations.
*   **Findings:** Extracted from the `reach_map.csv` generated by this script.
*   **Quantitative Proof:** "Top-down" success is found primarily in the $X \in [0.14, 0.22]$ range. This data forms the backbone of the "Evaluation" chapter, proving why the navigation must stop at exactly 0.22 m.

## 4. Final Controller Schema (`mycobot_controllers.yaml`)
*   **Update Rate:** 100 Hz.
*   **Control Type:** `JointTrajectoryController` for the arm (smooth paths) vs. `JointGroupPositionController` for the gripper (direct, low-latency clamping).
*   **Synchronicity:** All 6 gripper joints are commanded as a single `Float64MultiArray`, ensuring they move in unison despite lacking a physical mimic link.
